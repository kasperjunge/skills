# Hedgehog Concept

From *Good to Great*: the Hedgehog Concept is a simple, crystalline understanding of what an organization should focus on, found at the intersection of three circles. Collins treats the flywheel as a way to turn the Hedgehog Concept into practice, and uses the three circles to test a flywheel.

## The three circles

| Circle | Question |
|---|---|
| **Best in the world** | What can we be the best in the world at, and what can we not be the best at? |
| **Passion** | What are we deeply passionate about? |
| **Economic engine** | What best drives our economic engine? |

## Economic denominator

For the economic engine, Collins asks for a single denominator: "If you could pick one and only one ratio, profit per X (or cash flow per X), to systematically increase over time, what X would have the greatest and most sustainable impact on your economic engine?" Examples: profit per customer visit, profit per region, profit per employee.

## Principles

- **Understanding, not a goal.** The Hedgehog Concept is not a goal to be best; it is an understanding of what you *can* be best at.
- **Not what you're good at today.** Being good at something is not the same as being able to be the best in the world at it.
- **Discovered over time.** Good-to-great companies took years of iterative discussion to arrive at it.

## Testing a flywheel against the circles

- Does the flywheel play to what you can be best in the world at?
- Does it engage what people are deeply passionate about?
- Does turning it drive the economic denominator?

If a component is outside the three circles, it may be a distraction rather than a source of momentum.
