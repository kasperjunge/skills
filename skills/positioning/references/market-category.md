# Market category

The market category is a shortcut. Once customers know what category a product is in, they assume who the competitors are, what features it has, who it is for, and roughly what it costs. Choosing a category means choosing which assumptions customers bring.

## Choosing a category

- List several candidate categories, including ones that seem odd at first.
- For each, write down the assumptions it triggers: competitors, expected features, price range, buyer.
- Keep the categories whose assumptions put your unique value at the centre and make your weaknesses irrelevant.
- Prefer a category that best-fit customers already understand and search for.

## Three category styles

**1. Head to head.** Compete in an existing category and aim to win it. The category already exists, so customers understand it quickly. This works when you are, or can credibly claim to be, the leader, or when the category is young and nobody leads yet. It is hard for a newcomer against an established leader, because customers judge by the leader's criteria.

**2. Big fish, small pond.** Pick a sub-segment of an existing category where your strengths matter most, and win there. For example "project management for construction firms" instead of "project management". Customers understand the parent category, and the sub-segment explains why you are different. This is the most common winning style for challengers.

**3. Create a new game.** Define a new category when no existing one fits your value. This is the most expensive and slowest style: you must teach the market that the problem exists, that the category is the answer, and that you are the best in it. It works only when existing categories would seriously misrepresent the product, and when you have the resources and time to educate the market.

## Signals of a bad category fit

- Prospects keep asking for features that are standard in the category but irrelevant to your value.
- You lose on checklists against category leaders.
- Analysts or review sites put you in a category you don't recognise.
- Price expectations don't match your value.

## Failure modes

- **Creating a category by default.** Inventing a new category because it feels distinctive, without the resources to establish it.
- **Too broad a pond.** Competing head to head with leaders you can't beat on their terms.
- **Clever names.** A category name nobody searches for or understands without explanation.
