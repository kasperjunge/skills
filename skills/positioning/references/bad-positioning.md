# Bad positioning

Use this as a checklist when reviewing a positioning draft or existing messaging.

## Traps

- **Messaging without positioning.** A tagline or homepage with no agreed components behind it.
- **Alternatives that customers don't use.** Competitors named because they are well known, not because best-fit customers would choose them.
- **Doing nothing left out.** The status quo is missing from the alternatives.
- **Absolute attributes.** "Easy to use", "fast", "secure" without saying better than what.
- **Features presented as value.** Listing what the product does instead of what it is worth to the customer.
- **Value without proof.**
- **Everyone is the target.** Best-fit customers described so broadly that marketing can't find them.
- **Demographics instead of characteristics.** "Mid-size companies" instead of what makes them care.
- **Category chosen first.** The rest of the positioning reverse-engineered from a category someone liked.
- **A category that hides the strengths.** Its assumptions make your best attributes irrelevant.
- **A new category without the means to establish it.**
- **Trend-washing.** A trend layered on without a connection to the value.
- **Aspirational positioning.** Describing the product the team plans to build.
- **Inconsistent use.** Sales, marketing, and product each describe the product differently.

## Review questions

1. Is each component based on evidence from customers who love the product?
2. Would best-fit customers recognise the competitive alternatives as their real options?
3. Is each unique attribute stated relative to the alternatives?
4. Does each value theme have proof?
5. Can marketing and sales find best-fit customers from the description?
6. Does the market category put the product's strengths at the centre?
7. Does each component follow from the one before it?
8. Would the sales, marketing, and product teams describe the product this way?

## Before and after

| Bad | Better |
|---|---|
| "The all-in-one, AI-powered platform for modern teams." | "Scheduling software for multi-location clinics. Unlike spreadsheets and front-desk phone calls, it fills cancelled slots automatically, so clinics recover about 8% of lost appointments." |
| Competitors: Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft. | Alternatives: shared spreadsheets, a part-time admin, doing nothing. |
| Target: SMBs. | Target: agencies with 10–50 people that bill hourly and lose revenue to untracked time. |
