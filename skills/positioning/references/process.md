# The positioning process

Dunford's ten-step process from *Obviously Awesome*. It is a team exercise, usually done over a few working sessions.

1. **Understand the customers who love your product.** Find the customers who got the most value, bought fastest, and stay longest. They are the source of truth for every later step. If you don't have them yet, positioning is a hypothesis.
2. **Form a positioning team.** Include the people who own the business, marketing, sales, product, and customer success, and the CEO or founder. Positioning made by marketing alone doesn't get used by the rest of the company.
3. **Align your vocabulary and let go of positioning baggage.** Agree on what positioning means and what the components are. Acknowledge that the current positioning may be an accident of history, and that the product may have changed.
4. **List your true competitive alternatives.** What would your best customers do without you?
5. **Isolate your unique attributes.** What do you have that the alternatives lack?
6. **Map the attributes to value themes.** Attribute → benefit → value, then cluster into a small set of themes.
7. **Determine who cares a lot.** Describe the best-fit customer characteristics that make the value matter.
8. **Find a market frame of reference that puts your strengths at the centre, and decide how to position in it.** Try several categories, check which assumptions each one triggers, and pick a category style (head-to-head, big fish in a small pond, or a new category).
9. **Layer on a trend, carefully.** Optional. Only if it makes the value more urgent for best-fit customers.
10. **Capture your positioning so it can be shared.** Write it down, for example in a positioning canvas, and use it as the input to messaging, sales, and product. See the [positioning record](../record/structure.md).

## Running the exercise with an agent

- Ground every step in evidence: interviews, win/loss notes, sales calls, churn reasons, usage data. Mark anything inferred as an assumption.
- Do steps 4–7 in order. Skipping ahead to the category is the most common mistake.
- Generate several candidate categories at step 8 and test each against the components before choosing.
- End with open questions for the team. Positioning needs the team's agreement, not just a good draft.

## Failure modes

- **No customers who love the product yet.** Positioning then rests on guesses. Treat the output as a hypothesis and test it in sales conversations.
- **Running it without the people who will use it.** Sales and product ignore positioning they didn't help shape.
- **Treating the output as copy.** The canvas is an internal decision, not website text. Messaging comes later.
