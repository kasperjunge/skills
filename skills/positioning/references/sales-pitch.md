# Sales pitch

In *Sales Pitch*, Dunford turns positioning into a sales narrative. The pitch is not a product tour. It helps buyers make a decision they find hard, so it starts from their situation and the choice in front of them.

## Why buyers don't buy

Most lost deals are lost to **no decision**, not to a competitor. Buyers are afraid of making the wrong choice. A good pitch reduces that fear by making the decision feel clear and safe. This matches the anxiety and habit forces in the `jtbd` skill.

## Pitch structure

1. **Insight.** A point of view on how the market or the buyer's situation has changed, and why that matters now. It shows you understand the buyer's world and sets up the rest of the pitch.
2. **Alternatives and their limitations.** The competitive alternatives from the positioning, and why each falls short given the insight. Be fair; buyers know them.
3. **The perfect solution.** What an ideal solution would need to do, given the insight and the limits of the alternatives. Describe it without naming the product yet.
4. **Introduce the product.** Name the product and its market category, positioned as the way to reach that ideal.
5. **Value themes, with proof.** Walk through the two to four value themes from the positioning. For each, show proof: a demo, a customer story, a number.
6. **Objection handling.** Address the common concerns directly, before buyers raise them.
7. **Next steps.** Make the next step specific and low-risk.

## Using it

- Build the pitch from the positioning record, not from the feature list.
- Each part of the pitch should trace back to a component: insight to the trend and the category, alternatives to competitive alternatives, value themes to value and proof.
- The same structure works for a deck, a landing page, a demo script, or an investor pitch, with different levels of detail.

## Failure modes

- **Starting with the company or product.** Buyers care about their situation first.
- **Feature tours.** Showing everything the product does instead of the value themes.
- **Unsupported claims.** Value themes without proof.
- **Attacking alternatives.** Dismissing what the buyer uses today makes them defensive.
