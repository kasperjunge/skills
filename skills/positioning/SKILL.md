# Positioning

These concepts help you decide how a product is positioned and check whether that positioning holds up. They are drawn from April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* and *Sales Pitch*. Positioning is the **context** a product is understood in. It defines how the product is the best in the world at delivering some value that a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about. Customers use context to make sense of a product: when they see it, they ask what it is, what it is like, and who it is for. If you don't set that context deliberately, customers use a default one, often the wrong one. A great product in the wrong context looks mediocre.

Positioning has five components plus one optional layer: **competitive alternatives**, **unique attributes**, **value** (and proof), **best-fit customers**, and **market category**, with an optional **relevant trend**. The order matters: each component is derived from the one before it. Messaging, sales pitches, pricing, and launch plans all build on positioning, so position first.

Positioning works with the `jtbd` skill. Competitive alternatives are what customers would hire instead, and best-fit customers are the ones whose circumstance makes the job urgent. It is part of how a strategy creates advantage in the sense of the `strategy-kernel` skill.
