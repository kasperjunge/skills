# Flywheel

These concepts help you understand and design how an organization builds momentum. They are drawn from Jim Collins's monograph *Turning the Flywheel* and the flywheel chapter of *Good to Great*. The core idea: no great outcome comes from a single defining action, big program, or lucky break. It comes from turning a flywheel, a small set of components in which each step leads almost inevitably to the next, and the last feeds back into the first. Each turn builds on the previous ones, until momentum carries the organization forward. The opposite is the **doom loop**: lurching from one new direction to another, never building momentum.

The flywheel is an architecture of momentum, not a strategy on its own. It works when its components follow from a clear understanding of what you can be best at (the [Hedgehog Concept](references/hedgehog-concept.md)), and it is a source of power in the sense of the `strategy-kernel` skill. It is how an organization moves toward the BHAG in the `vision` skill, and the `okrs` skill can measure how well each component is turning.
