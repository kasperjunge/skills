# Bad flywheels

Use this as a checklist when reviewing a draft flywheel.

## Traps

- **A list, not a loop.** Priorities or values arranged in a circle, without each causing the next.
- **The loop doesn't close.** The last component doesn't feed back into the first, so nothing compounds.
- **Too many components.** More than six, so it no longer captures the essence.
- **Aspirational.** Built from what you wish would drive momentum, not from what your successes and disappointments show.
- **Generic.** Could describe any company in the industry.
- **Outside the three circles.** Components that don't play to what you can be best at, what you're passionate about, or your economic engine.
- **Activities, not outcomes.** "Run campaigns" or "hold workshops" instead of what those produce.
- **Confused with a strategy.** A flywheel describes how momentum compounds; it doesn't diagnose the challenge you face now. See the `strategy-kernel` skill.
- **Doom loop behaviour.** Replacing the flywheel each year with a new program.

## Review questions

1. Does each component lead almost inevitably to the next?
2. Does the last component feed back into the first?
3. Are there four to six components?
4. Does it explain past successes and disappointments?
5. Does it pass the three circles test?
6. Is it specific to this organization?
7. Can each component be turned into concrete actions and metrics?

## Before and after

| Bad | Better |
|---|---|
| Great product → Great marketing → Great people → Great culture → Great product | Fix one painful job for small agencies → Agencies succeed and refer peers → Lower cost to acquire → More revenue reinvested in product depth → Fix the job even better |
| Twelve components covering every department. | Five components capturing how momentum actually builds. |
