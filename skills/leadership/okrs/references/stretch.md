# Stretch

Superpower 4: **stretch for amazing**. OKRs can push teams beyond what they think is possible. Doerr cites Grove: setting goals beyond the easily reachable produces better results, even when the goal is missed.

## Committed vs. aspirational

Google separates two kinds of OKRs, and every OKR should be clearly labelled as one or the other.

| | Committed | Aspirational (stretch) |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Things the team agrees will get done. | Bigger bets: where the team would like to be. |
| **Expected score** | 1.0. Less is a failure to be explained. | Average around 0.7. Misses are expected. |
| **Resourcing** | Adjust schedules and resources to deliver. | Pursued with what is available. |
| **Examples** | Ship SSO by week 8. Keep uptime at 99.9%. | 10x reduction in onboarding time. |

## Principles

- **Make stretch real.** An aspirational OKR should be uncomfortable but believable. If nobody thinks it's possible, it demoralizes rather than motivates.
- **Leaders set the tone.** Teams only stretch when missing a stretch goal is safe.
- **Think 10x.** Google's moonshot thinking: a 10x goal forces a different approach, where a 10% goal invites incremental work.
- **Not everything should stretch.** Operational commitments that others depend on must be committed OKRs.

## Failure modes

- **Unlabelled OKRs.** Nobody knows whether 0.7 is success or failure.
- **Timid aspirations.** "Aspirational" OKRs that are really business as usual.
- **Sandbagging.** Teams set easy committed OKRs to guarantee 1.0, usually because scores are tied to evaluation or pay.
- **Stretch without belief.** Impossible goals that teams quietly give up on.
