# Focus

Superpower 1: **focus and commit to priorities**. OKRs force leaders to decide what matters most, and just as important, what doesn't.

## Principles

- **Fewer is more.** Three to five objectives per cycle, each with no more than five key results. Too many OKRs dilute effort and hide what matters.
- **Choose and drop.** Committing to some objectives means saying no to others. If nothing was dropped, nothing was prioritized.
- **Communicate the why.** Leaders must explain why each objective matters, not only what it is. Clarity of purpose lets people make good decisions without asking.
- **Set the time frame.** Quarterly OKRs keep pace with fast-moving work; annual OKRs hold longer-term direction. Many organizations use both.
- **Commit.** Once set, leaders publicly commit to the OKRs and model that commitment.

## Questions to sharpen focus

- What are the most important things to achieve in the next three months?
- If we achieve only one of these, which one?
- What are we explicitly not doing this cycle?
- Would anyone notice if this objective were removed?

## Failure modes

- **Everything is a priority.** Ten objectives means none of them is.
- **Business as usual.** Objectives that describe ongoing work rather than meaningful change.
- **Unexplained objectives.** People don't know why an objective matters, so they can't trade off well.
