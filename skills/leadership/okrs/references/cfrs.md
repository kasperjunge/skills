# CFRs

Doerr pairs OKRs with **CFRs**: **conversations**, **feedback**, and **recognition**. OKRs set *what* to achieve; CFRs shape *how* people work together toward it. Together they support **continuous performance management**, replacing or supplementing the annual review.

## The three parts

- **Conversations.** Regular one-on-ones between manager and contributor about goals, progress, obstacles, coaching, and growth. The contributor sets much of the agenda.
- **Feedback.** Two-way and peer-to-peer communication about how work is going, given close to the moment rather than saved for a review.
- **Recognition.** Expressing appreciation for contributions, large and small, frequently, publicly, and tied to company goals.

## Decouple OKRs from compensation

Doerr argues, following Grove, that OKRs should not be used directly to set pay or bonuses.

- When pay depends on hitting OKRs, people set safe goals and stretch disappears.
- OKRs are not a legal document or an employee evaluation tool. Performance assessment should also consider how goals were set, teamwork, and contributions beyond the OKRs.
- OKRs can still inform a performance conversation, as one input among several.

## Culture

OKRs and CFRs work only in a culture of transparency, accountability, and trust. Doerr's view: culture comes first; OKRs reinforce a healthy culture but cannot fix a broken one on their own.

## Failure modes

- **OKRs as a scorecard for pay.** Leads to sandbagging and gaming.
- **Annual-only reviews.** Feedback arrives too late to change anything.
- **Recognition only at the top.** Contributors don't see their work acknowledged.
