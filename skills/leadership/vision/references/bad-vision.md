# Bad vision

Use this as a checklist when reviewing a vision, mission, purpose, or values statement.

## Traps

- **Confusing core and non-core.** Treating practices, strategies, products, or cultural norms as core ideology. They then become impossible to change, or the real core is lost when they do.
- **Writing it by committee.** Wordsmithing a statement for its own sake. Collins and Porras stress that the content matters, not the wording, and that a vision is discovered and lived, not drafted.
- **Aspirational values.** Values that describe who you wish you were rather than who you are.
- **Purpose as product.** A "mission" that describes what you sell to whom.
- **Purpose as profit.** Making money is necessary, not a reason for being.
- **Fuzzy future.** "Be the best" or "lead the industry" with no finish line or picture.
- **No ideology.** A bold goal with nothing stable underneath, so the organization drifts with each new goal.
- **Vision as strategy.** A vision says where you want to go, not how to overcome the obstacles on the way. Rumelt calls goals posing as strategy bad strategy. See the `strategy-kernel` skill.
- **Not lived.** The statement exists on a wall but doesn't shape hiring, decisions, or incentives.

## Review questions

1. Are core values few, authentic, and kept even if they became a disadvantage?
2. Is the purpose a reason for being that could last 100 years and never be finished?
3. Is there a BHAG with a clear finish line, 10–30 years out?
4. Is there a vivid description that makes the BHAG concrete?
5. Is everything in the core ideology truly unchanging, and everything else open to change?
6. Is there a separate strategy for getting there?

## Before and after

| Bad | Better |
|---|---|
| "Our mission is to be the leading provider of innovative software solutions." | **Purpose:** Help small businesses spend their time on the work they love. **BHAG:** One million small businesses run their entire back office on us by 2040. |
| **Values:** Integrity, excellence, innovation, teamwork, passion, customer focus, agility. | **Values:** Tell the truth, especially when it's uncomfortable. Customers' time is sacred. |
