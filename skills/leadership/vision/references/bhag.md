# BHAG

A **Big Hairy Audacious Goal** is the first part of the **envisioned future**. It is a clear, compelling, long-term goal that unifies effort and serves as a focal point. The [vivid description](vivid-description.md) is the second part.

## Principles

- **Long-term.** It takes 10 to 30 years of effort to achieve.
- **Clear finish line.** People know when it has been reached. Unlike the purpose, a BHAG can be completed.
- **Audacious but possible.** Collins and Porras suggest roughly a 50–70% probability of success. It requires extraordinary effort and perhaps luck.
- **Engaging.** It reaches out and grabs people, needing little explanation.
- **Consistent with the core.** It expresses the core ideology rather than contradicting it.

## Types of BHAG

| Type | Example (cited by Collins and Porras) |
|---|---|
| **Target** (quantitative or qualitative) | Walmart (1990): become a $125 billion company by the year 2000. Kennedy: land a man on the moon and return him safely before the decade is out. |
| **Common enemy** | Nike (1960s): crush Adidas. |
| **Role model** | Stanford (1940s): become the Harvard of the West. |
| **Internal transformation** | Rockwell (1995): transform from a defense contractor into the best diversified high-tech company in the world. |

Common-enemy and role-model BHAGs often suit younger organizations; internal transformation suits large established ones.

## Tests for a BHAG

- Would it take 10–30 years?
- Is it clear when it has been achieved?
- Does it make people uncomfortable yet believe it is possible?
- Does it need little explanation to be understood and remembered?
- Would achieving it be consistent with the core ideology?

## After the BHAG

Once achieved, a BHAG must be replaced. Collins and Porras warn of the "we've arrived" syndrome: complacency sets in when the organization reaches its goal without setting a new one.

## Failure modes

- **Too safe.** A goal achievable with ordinary effort is a plan target, not a BHAG.
- **Too fuzzy.** "Be world-class" has no finish line.
- **Too short.** A two-year goal is a strategic objective; see the `okrs` and `strategy-kernel` skills.
- **No commitment.** A BHAG stated once and then ignored.
