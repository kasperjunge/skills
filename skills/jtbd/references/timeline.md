# The buying timeline

Moesta maps how a person moves from first struggle to using a new solution. People don't decide in a single moment. They pass through phases, and specific events move them from one phase to the next. Knowing the phase a person is in tells you what they need.

## The phases

1. **First thought**: the person first notices that something isn't working. Often a struggling moment they dismiss at first. Nothing is done yet.
2. **Passive looking**: the problem is on their mind. They notice solutions, ads, and what others use, but don't spend time or money looking. This phase can last months or years.
3. **Active looking**: an event makes the problem pressing. Now they invest time: researching, comparing, asking others, trying things. They are defining what "better" means.
4. **Deciding**: another event (a deadline, a budget, a trigger) forces a choice. They make trade-offs and pick something, including possibly doing nothing.
5. **First use**: they start using the new solution and compare it to what they expected.
6. **Ongoing use**: the solution either becomes part of how they make progress (the little hire succeeds) or gets fired.

## Events

Movement between phases is triggered by events, not by gradual pressure. Common events: a deadline, a failure, a change in role or life, a conversation with someone, a price or plan change, a moment of embarrassment. Events are the clearest signals of the job, because they show what made the struggle matter enough to act.

## Using the timeline

- **Different phases need different things.** Passive lookers need to recognize their struggle; active lookers need help defining and comparing options; deciders need anxiety reduced; new users need early proof that the job is getting done.
- **Most of the story happens before the product is involved.** The circumstances and events that create demand happen long before a purchase.
- **Demand is created by struggle, not by sales.** Sales and marketing can help people make progress through the timeline, but can't create the push.

## Failure modes

- **Treating the purchase as the start.** Ignoring the first thought and passive looking, where the job is most visible.
- **One message for every phase.** Pitching features to someone who hasn't yet recognized the problem.
- **Stopping at the decision.** Not following the person into first and ongoing use, where the little hire is won or lost.
