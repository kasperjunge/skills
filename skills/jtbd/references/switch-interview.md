# Switch interviews

The switch interview is Moesta's method for uncovering jobs. It reconstructs the story of a real, recent switch: someone who hired a new solution, or fired an old one. The goal is to understand the circumstances, events, and [forces](forces-of-progress.md) behind the decision, not opinions about the product.

## Principles

- **Interview recent switchers.** People who switched within the last few months remember the details. Include people who switched away from the product, not just those who switched to it.
- **Ask about what happened, not what they want.** Hypothetical questions ("Would you use…?", "What features do you want?") produce guesses. Past behavior is evidence.
- **Work like a documentary maker.** Reconstruct the scene: where they were, what time it was, who was there, what they said. Concrete details trigger real memories and surface the emotional and social dimensions.
- **Work backward and forward from the purchase.** Start at the moment of buying, then go back to the first thought, then forward to first and ongoing use. See [the buying timeline](timeline.md).
- **Follow energy and contradictions.** When the person hesitates, laughs, or contradicts themselves, there is usually a force at work. Dig in.
- **Don't sell or defend.** The interviewer is there to understand, not to explain the product.

## Structure

1. **The purchase**: When did you buy it? Where were you? Who else was involved?
2. **First thought**: When did you first realize you needed something different? What was going on?
3. **Passive looking**: What did you notice in the meantime? What did you do instead?
4. **Event**: What happened that made you start looking seriously?
5. **Active looking**: What did you consider? How did you compare? What did "better" mean to you?
6. **Deciding**: What made you decide then? What almost stopped you? What did you give up?
7. **Use**: What was the first time using it like? Was it what you expected? Do you still use it?

## Making sense of interviews

After roughly 10–20 interviews, patterns emerge. Group interviews by similar circumstances and forces, not by demographics. Each cluster is a candidate job. Write a [job statement](the-job.md) for each, and list its push, pull, anxieties, habits, and the events that triggered action.

## Failure modes

- **Asking for opinions.** "What do you like about the product?" produces reviews, not jobs.
- **Interviewing the wrong people.** Long-time users who can't remember why they switched, or people who never switched.
- **Leading questions.** Suggesting answers that confirm what the team already believes.
- **Stopping at the first answer.** People give rational, socially acceptable reasons first. The real reasons come with follow-up.
- **Clustering by persona.** Grouping by who people are rather than by the circumstance and progress they describe.
