# Critical chain

In *Critical Chain*, Goldratt applies constraints thinking to projects. Projects run late even though every task estimate contains safety time. Critical chain explains why and moves the safety to where it protects the project.

## Why projects run late

- **Padded estimates.** People add safety to each task, because they are judged on finishing on time.
- **Student syndrome.** With safety built in, work starts late.
- **Parkinson's law.** Work expands to fill the time available; early finishes are not reported, so they are lost.
- **Multitasking.** People switch between projects, so every task takes longer, and every project is late.
- **Dependent events.** As in [The goal and its measures](goal-and-measures.md): delays pass down the chain, early finishes don't.

## The critical chain

The **critical chain** is the longest chain of dependent tasks, taking both task dependencies and resource dependencies into account. Unlike the critical path, it recognises that one person or team can't do two tasks at once.

## Buffers

- Cut task estimates to an aggressive but possible duration, removing the hidden safety.
- Put the safety back as a **project buffer** at the end of the critical chain, about half the removed safety.
- Add **feeding buffers** where non-critical chains join the critical chain, so delays there don't delay it.
- Use **resource buffers**: alert resources on the critical chain before they are needed.
- Track progress with a **fever chart**: buffer consumed against chain completed. Act when buffer use outpaces progress.

## Multiple projects

- Find the resource that most projects depend on. It is the drum for the portfolio.
- Stagger project start dates according to that resource's capacity.
- Stop starting, start finishing: fewer projects in progress finish sooner on average.

## Failure modes

- **Cutting estimates without a project buffer.** Turns a scheduling method into pressure.
- **Holding people to task estimates.** Defeats the purpose; only the buffer is managed.
- **Starting every approved project at once.**
