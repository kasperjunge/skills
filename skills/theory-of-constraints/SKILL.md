# Theory of Constraints

These concepts help you improve a system as a whole rather than part by part. They are drawn from Eliyahu Goldratt's *The Goal*, *It's Not Luck*, and *Critical Chain*. The core idea: every system that has a goal has at least one **constraint**, the thing that limits how much of the goal it can reach. The output of the whole system is set by its constraint. An hour lost at the constraint is an hour lost for the whole system; an hour saved anywhere else changes nothing. So improving a system means finding its constraint, getting the most out of it, and making everything else serve it, before spending on more capacity.

Constraints are not always machines or people. Often they are **policies**: rules, measures, and habits that made sense once and now limit the system. The thinking processes exist to find and remove those.
