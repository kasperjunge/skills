# Alignment

Superpower 2: **align and connect for teamwork**. When OKRs are public, everyone can see how their work connects to the company's goals and to each other.

## Principles

- **Transparency.** Everyone's OKRs, from the CEO down, are visible to everyone. This exposes duplicated effort, gaps, and misalignment.
- **Cascading.** A higher-level key result can become a lower-level objective, so goals connect from company to team to individual.
- **Not only top-down.** Purely cascaded OKRs are rigid and slow and strip teams of ownership. Doerr suggests roughly half of OKRs be set bottom-up, by the people doing the work.
- **Horizontal connection.** Many dependencies run across teams, not up and down. Make them explicit and agree on shared OKRs where teams depend on each other.

## Cascading example

| Level | Objective | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Company | Grow revenue in the mid-market. | 40 new mid-market customers. |
| Sales | Land 40 new mid-market customers. | 120 qualified demos. |
| Product | Make the product ready for mid-market. | SSO and audit logs shipped by week 8. |

## Questions to check alignment

- Can each team trace its OKRs to a company objective?
- Which teams depend on each other to hit their key results, and have they agreed?
- Which OKRs did teams propose themselves?
- Are two teams pursuing conflicting key results?

## Failure modes

- **Mechanical cascading.** Every OKR is handed down, so teams feel no ownership and the system can't adapt.
- **Hidden dependencies.** A team's key result depends on another team that never agreed to it.
- **Private OKRs.** Goals that nobody else can see can't be aligned with.
