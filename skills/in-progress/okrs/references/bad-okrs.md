# Bad OKRs

Use this as a checklist when reviewing a draft. Several traps come from Google's OKR guidance, reproduced in *Measure What Matters*.

## Traps

- **Not separating committed from aspirational.** Unlabelled OKRs make scores meaningless and plans unreliable.
- **Business-as-usual OKRs.** Describing what the team would do anyway, rather than what would change.
- **Timid aspirational OKRs.** Starting from current state and adding a little, instead of asking what customers or the business would really want.
- **Sandbagging.** Committed OKRs that are guaranteed to hit 1.0 without real effort.
- **Low-value objectives.** Objectives that could be fully achieved without anyone caring. Ask: would the business or customer notice?
- **Insufficient key results.** Achieving all key results would still not achieve the objective.
- **Key results as tasks.** "Launch campaign" or "hold workshops" describe activity, not outcomes.
- **Unmeasurable key results.** No number, no deadline, or no way to verify.
- **Too many OKRs.** More than about five objectives, or more than five key results per objective.
- **Unpaired metrics.** A quantity key result with nothing guarding quality.
- **Purely top-down.** Teams were handed OKRs with no say, so they don't own them.
- **Set and forget.** No check-ins until the end of the cycle.
- **Tied to pay.** Scores feed directly into bonuses, so people set safe goals.
- **No strategy behind them.** A list of targets that doesn't follow from a diagnosis of the challenge. See the `strategy-kernel` skill.

## Review questions

1. Is each OKR labelled committed or aspirational?
2. Is the objective significant and clear about why it matters?
3. Would achieving all key results mean the objective is achieved?
4. Is every key result a measurable outcome with a number and a deadline?
5. Are quantity key results paired with quality ones?
6. Are there few enough OKRs to focus on?
7. Do they connect to the company's OKRs and to dependent teams?
8. Do they follow from the strategy?

## Before and after

| Bad | Better |
|---|---|
| **O:** Improve marketing. **KR:** Launch new website. | **O:** Make inbound our main source of pipeline. **KR:** 300 inbound qualified leads per month (from 120). |
| **O:** Be customer-centric. **KR:** Run customer interviews. | **O:** Cut churn among small accounts. **KR:** Monthly churn below 2% (from 4%). |
| **O:** Ship v2. | **O:** Make v2 the version customers choose. **KR:** 70% of active accounts on v2 by end of quarter. |
