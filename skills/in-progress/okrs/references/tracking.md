# Tracking

Superpower 3: **track for accountability**. OKRs are a living system, checked regularly, adjusted when needed, and scored at the end of the cycle.

## During the cycle

- **Regular check-ins.** Review progress weekly or at least regularly between a person and their manager, and in team meetings.
- **Adjust openly.** At any point an OKR can be:
  - **Continued**: on track, keep going.
  - **Updated**: the key result or objective needs changing to reflect reality.
  - **Started**: a new OKR is needed.
  - **Stopped**: the OKR is no longer worth pursuing.
- Changing OKRs mid-cycle is fine. Pretending a stale OKR still matters is not.

## End of cycle

Score each key result from 0.0 to 1.0, then average for the objective.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0.7–1.0 | Green: delivered. |
| 0.4–0.6 | Yellow: progress, but fell short. |
| 0.0–0.3 | Red: failed to make real progress. |

Committed and aspirational OKRs are judged differently; see [Stretch](stretch.md).

## Reflection

Scoring is followed by self-assessment and reflection:

- Did I accomplish all of my objectives? If so, what contributed to success?
- If not, what obstacles did I encounter?
- If I were to rewrite an achieved goal, what would I change?
- What have I learned that might alter my approach to the next cycle's OKRs?

## Failure modes

- **Set and forget.** OKRs are written in week 1 and not seen again until the review.
- **Silent abandonment.** An OKR stops mattering but is never formally stopped.
- **Scoring as judgment.** Scores used to rank or punish people, which encourages sandbagging.
