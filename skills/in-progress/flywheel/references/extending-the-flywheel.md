# Extending the flywheel

A well-built flywheel can last for decades, but it must be continually renewed and extended. Collins describes two opposite errors: abandoning a good flywheel too soon, and keeping it turning unchanged when it needs to be renewed.

## Principles

- **Stay with it.** A flywheel compounds only over many turns. Most organizations give up on it too early, chasing the next new thing.
- **Innovate within it.** Renew each component with new products, practices, and technologies, while the underlying architecture stays the same.
- **Extend into new arenas.** Apply the same flywheel to new markets, segments, or offerings, as long as they fit the architecture. Amazon extended its flywheel into new product categories and to third-party sellers.
- **Separate the flywheel from specific bets.** A product line or channel can die without the flywheel dying. Don't confuse the vehicle with the architecture.
- **Fire bullets, then cannonballs.** From *Great by Choice*: test new extensions with small, low-cost experiments, and commit big resources only once there is empirical evidence they work.

## Questions

- Is the flywheel losing momentum, or is a specific product or market losing momentum?
- Which component most needs renewal?
- Where else could the same flywheel turn?
- Does the proposed new direction fit the flywheel, or would it start a new one from scratch?

## Failure modes

- **Abandoning too early.** Switching direction before momentum builds: the start of a doom loop.
- **Running it unchanged.** Doing what worked yesterday until the market moves on.
- **Extensions that don't fit.** Expanding into areas that don't turn the flywheel, diluting momentum.
- **Untested big bets.** Committing heavily to an extension before small experiments prove it.
