# Building a flywheel

In *Turning the Flywheel*, Collins lays out a process for articulating your flywheel. It is discovered from what has actually worked, not invented from what you wish would work.

## Steps

1. **List successes.** Write down significant, replicable successes the organization has achieved.
2. **List disappointments.** Write down failures and disappointments.
3. **Compare them.** Ask what the comparison reveals about the possible components of the flywheel.
4. **Pick the components.** Choose four to six components that capture what drives momentum. Fewer is better; more than six becomes too complex to be useful.
5. **Sequence them.** Decide where the flywheel starts (the top) and the order of what follows. Each component should lead almost inevitably to the next.
6. **Close the loop.** The last component must feed back into the first, so the flywheel turns.
7. **Simplify.** Consolidate components until the flywheel captures the essence in a few words.
8. **Test it.** Check it against the lists of successes and disappointments: does it explain them? Then test it against the three circles of the [Hedgehog Concept](hedgehog-concept.md).

## The "this, then almost inevitably that" test

Read the flywheel aloud: "We do A, which leads to B, which leads to C, which leads back to A." If you can't explain why each step follows from the previous one, the sequence is wrong or a component is missing.

## Components

- **Causal, not a list.** Each component causes the next. A list of priorities or values is not a flywheel.
- **Specific to you.** It should capture the particular logic of your organization, not a generic business model.
- **Durable.** Components are general enough to last a decade or more, but specific enough to guide decisions.
- **Usable at any level.** A company, business unit, or team can each have a flywheel, as long as it connects to the larger one.

## Using it

- Translate each component into concrete actions and metrics.
- Use it to decide what to do and what not to do: if an initiative doesn't turn the flywheel, question it.
- Review it periodically, but expect the core architecture to hold for years.
