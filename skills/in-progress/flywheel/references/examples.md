# Examples

These examples are paraphrased from cases Collins discusses. They illustrate the shape of a flywheel: few components, each causing the next, closing back on itself.

## Amazon

Collins worked with Amazon's leadership on their flywheel, famously sketched by Jeff Bezos on a napkin.

1. Lower prices on more offerings.
2. More customer visits.
3. More volume, which attracts third-party sellers.
4. More selection and more revenue per fixed cost (fulfilment centres, servers).
5. Lower cost structure, which funds lower prices, back to step 1.

## Vanguard

1. Lower costs for investors.
2. Better long-term returns relative to comparable funds.
3. More investor loyalty and trust.
4. More assets under management.
5. More scale, which drives lower costs, back to step 1.

## Illustrative: B2B software company

Not from the book; shows the shape applied elsewhere.

1. Solve one painful job extremely well for a narrow segment.
2. Customers succeed and refer peers.
3. Lower acquisition cost per customer.
4. More revenue per customer acquired, reinvested in product depth.
5. Even better solution for the segment, back to step 1.

## What these have in common

- Four to six components.
- Each step causes the next, not just follows it.
- The loop closes: the last component strengthens the first.
- The components are grounded in what the organization can be best at and how it makes money.
