# Dimensions of a job

Jobs are never purely functional. Christensen describes three dimensions, and in many purchases the emotional and social dimensions outweigh the functional one.

## Functional

The practical problem to solve: getting from A to B, filing taxes, keeping food cold. Functional requirements are the easiest to state and measure, which is why teams tend to focus on them and stop there.

## Emotional

How the person wants to feel, or stop feeling: confident, safe, in control, less anxious, not stupid. Someone hiring an accountant may care less about the return itself than about not lying awake worrying about an audit.

## Social

How the person wants to be seen by others: as a responsible parent, a competent professional, someone with taste. A manager buying an established vendor's software may be hiring it partly so nobody can blame them if it fails.

## Why all three matter

- **They explain choices features can't.** Two products with the same functions win or lose on how they make people feel and look.
- **They reveal the real competition.** An emotional job ("feel productive on the commute") can be served by things that share no features with the product.
- **They shape the experience.** Requirements come from all three dimensions. The milkshake had to be thick (functional: lasts the commute) and something you'd be comfortable holding in the car (social).

## Experiences of purchase and use

Christensen stresses that people hire a whole experience, not just a product. The experiences of finding, buying, and using a solution must also fit the job. A product that does the functional job well but is hard to buy, set up, or trust will lose to one that is worse functionally but easier to hire.

## Questions that surface the dimensions

- What would getting this done let the person stop worrying about?
- How did they feel before, and how did they want to feel after?
- Who else is affected or watching? What would they think?
- What would be embarrassing or risky about choosing wrong?

## Failure modes

- **Functional only.** The job reads like a spec, with no emotional or social reasons.
- **Emotion as decoration.** Emotional dimensions are listed but don't shape requirements or messaging.
- **Stated, not observed.** People rarely volunteer emotional and social reasons. They show up in what people did, the words they use, and what they were anxious about.
