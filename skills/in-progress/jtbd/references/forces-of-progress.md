# Forces of progress

Moesta's model of why people switch, or don't. Four forces act on every decision to hire something new. Two push toward change; two hold people back. People switch only when the forces for change outweigh the forces against it.

## The four forces

**Toward change:**

1. **Push of the situation**: the struggle with the current way. Something isn't working, or a change in circumstance (new job, new baby, a failure, a deadline) makes the old way unacceptable. Without a push, people don't look.
2. **Pull of the new solution**: the attraction of a better future. The picture of how life will be once the new thing is hired. Pull without push rarely leads to action.

**Against change:**

3. **Anxiety of the new solution**: worries about the new thing. Will it work? Will I be able to use it? What if it's a waste of money? What will others think?
4. **Habit of the present**: attachment to the current way, even when it's poor. Familiarity, sunk investment, workarounds that are "good enough", and the effort of changing.

## Using the model

- **Reducing the forces against is often cheaper than increasing the forces for.** Teams tend to add features and marketing (more pull). Free trials, guarantees, migration help, and social proof reduce anxiety and habit, and often move more people.
- **Push can't be created, only found.** A product can't make people struggle; it can find people who already do, and reach them at the moment the push is strongest.
- **The balance is per circumstance.** The same person can be ready to switch in one circumstance and stuck in another.
- **Forces explain non-switching.** When people say "this looks great" but don't buy, the forces against are winning. Find out which.

## Questions that surface the forces

- **Push**: What was going on when you started looking? What happened that made the old way not good enough anymore?
- **Pull**: What did you imagine would be different once you had it?
- **Anxiety**: What almost stopped you? What were you worried about?
- **Habit**: What did you like about the old way? What did you have to give up or relearn?

## Failure modes

- **Only selling pull.** Messaging and roadmaps focused on features, ignoring the anxiety and habit that block the switch.
- **Targeting people without push.** Marketing to people who are satisfied with the current way.
- **Assuming switching is rational.** Habit and anxiety are emotional; a better product alone doesn't overcome them.
