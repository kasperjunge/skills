# Proximate objectives

A proximate objective is a target close enough to reach with the resources and knowledge available now. Rumelt treats it as one of the most important sources of power in strategy, because it turns an ambiguous situation into a problem people can actually solve.

## Why they matter

- **They resolve ambiguity.** In an uncertain situation, a leader's job is to pick a target that people can act on, absorbing the uncertainty so the team doesn't have to.
- **They focus energy.** A clear, reachable target concentrates effort instead of spreading it across vague aspirations.
- **They create learning.** Reaching a near-term objective produces results and information that sharpen the next step.
- **They build momentum.** Success on a feasible objective builds confidence and capability for harder ones.

## Example

When NASA planned the Moon landings, nobody knew what the lunar surface was like. Rather than leave designers stuck on that uncertainty, the program set a working assumption about the surface that engineers could design against. The uncertainty did not go away, but it was turned into a problem that could be solved.

Kennedy's goal of landing on the Moon within the decade was itself fairly proximate: much of the needed rocket technology was already understood. It was ambitious but feasible, which is very different from a goal that depends on breakthroughs nobody knows how to make.

## Proximate vs. aspirational

| Aspirational | Proximate |
|---|---|
| Be the leading platform in Europe. | Sign 20 paying customers in Denmark by March. |
| Build a world-class engineering culture. | Every team deploys to production daily by Q2. |
| Fix onboarding. | 60% of new users complete their first project within a week. |

## Tests for a proximate objective

- **Feasible**: it is known how to reach it, with the resources available. If not, break it down or set a smaller target.
- **Relevant**: reaching it moves toward the crux, not only toward something easy to measure.
- **Near**: results show within roughly 1–3 months.
- **Informative**: reaching it, or failing to, teaches something that shapes the next objective.

## Failure modes

- **Too far.** The objective needs unknown breakthroughs or resources that aren't available.
- **Too easy.** Reachable but irrelevant. It measures activity, not progress on the crux.
- **Stretch goals as strategy.** Big numbers with no idea of how to reach them. Rumelt sees this as a form of bad strategy.
- **Vanity metrics.** Easy to move but disconnected from the challenge.
