# Reframe

Change how we understand the problem before changing how we solve it.

1. Identify the current framing. State the question being asked and its underlying assumptions.
2. Offer 2–4 meaningfully different frames. Change the perspective, scope, timescale, or definition of success—not just the wording.
3. Explain what each frame reveals and what it leaves out.
4. Recommend the most useful frame for the user’s objective and state the new question it leads to.

## Principles

- Stay grounded in the actual situation.
- Explore without forcing the original framing to be wrong.
- Prefer a revealing perspective over a clever analogy.
- Surface new questions rather than jumping into solutions.

## Output

Keep it concise:

- **Current frame:** How are we seeing the problem?
- **Alternative frames:** What changes under each perspective?
- **Recommended frame:** Which is most useful, and why?
- **New question:** What should we ask now?
